As mentioned in the term project paper, the damping parameter did not seem to affect the overall behavior of the particles. When setting parameters for the simulation, it was initially assumed that a velocity comparable to the circular rotational velocity would generate a desirable outcome. But for some testing instances, setting particles' initial velocities to be similar to the circular rotational velocity did not generate a stable structure, as a heavy core did not seem to form. So a damping parameter was introduced, so that the particles would gradually lose their momentum and fall to the center to form a stable core. This introduction of a damping parameter was reasonable, because some celestial bodies surely lose their mechanical energy into heat, etc. The damping seemed to prompt the formation of core structures. Damping parameters between 0.995 and 0.8 (per 10 calculation steps) were tested, and values between 0.99 and 0.9 gave plausible behaviors. So this parameter was also taken into account in modeling the simulation.
 However, as shown in the paper, initial velocities near the circular rotational velocities are not suitable initial conditions for spiral structure. Since these velocities have generated awkward behaviors without the damping, it is reasonable to investigate the damping parameter at the suitable initial velocities (near zero, as in the paper). After a few tests of damping parameter values, it seemed that the overall behavior did not differ a lot when damping was restricted to values between 0.99 and 0.95. Hence, it could be concluded that the damping parameter was not an important factor in this simulation.
 It must be noted that this is only a qualitative demonstration of a phenomenon found during this project. More research of higher quality and quantity must be done to properly demonstrate and explain why this phenomenon occurred.
